Dissertation

Probabilistic and Generative Modeling for Emotion Recognition and Synthesis

Degree
PhD in Computer Science, University of Hamburg

Supervisors

Current AI systems still lack social intelligence: the ability to perceive and express the social signals that make collaboration work. The dissertation focuses on affect and studies it in three directions: recognising it in one person, recognising it in a group, and synthesising it in speech.

1Individual affect

  1. Bayesian networks trained on the full spread of annotations, not just their mean.Interspeech 2022IEEE TAFFC 2023
  2. Student's t-distributions instead of Gaussians, so fewer annotations mean a more uncertain label.ACII 2022
  3. Audio-textual distillation for efficient self-supervised emotion recognition.Interspeech 2023

2Group affect

  1. A psychology-grounded protocol for annotating the affect of a whole group over time.IEEE TAFFC 2026
  2. Why behavioural labels of groups are subjective and uncertain.OUP chapter 2025
  3. Multimodal models of how group members' emotions converge and diverge.IEEE TAFFC 2026

3Affect synthesis

  1. Emotion conversion without parallel data, by disentangling speaker, content and emotion.ITG 2023
  2. Diffusion decoders that reach rare, extreme arousal.ICASSP 2024
  3. Speech rate that follows arousal: slower when calm, faster when excited.IEEE ASRU 2025
